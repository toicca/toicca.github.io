🇬🇧 Why have a personal site and what to do with it.

Starting out
======

I have long thought about a personal site of some sort. Part of this interest comes from looking up senior researchers names (or sometimes juniors also), seeing their pages pop up, and feeling perhaps a need to imitate them one way or the other. Part of the interest comes from notekeeping and from having an archive of thoughts and things that I have done, it's a pain to update a CV based on memory or the previous iteration of the document and whatever software that was made with. There are also pragmatic reasons. By having this page I can be seen by others, something which seems more and more valuable as I compete in an international job market and try to build a personal brand. Maybe not something that would solely motivate me to write or to build a site, but something that can be valuable as a by-product of all of this.

It is not a coincidence that I start this now with the tools that I have and the environment I am in. The idea of a personal site started earlier than in 2026, but back then I hoped to have a different approach to making. Previously making a site wasn't as easy as it is now, which was to me a part of the attraction of having one. I hoped to learn more about the web, building sites, hosting, etc. and to have more of a developer experience with it than I'm currently having. The initial goal was not to have a copy-paste site hosted on Squarespace or similar and to just start producing content to that site, but I hoped to learn from the making of the site. This project of learning got to a relatively good start when I started to work on my github.io page, but as I started to study and look into things I found more and more tools ready-made for just what I needed. I didn't need to solve any problems, the templates were there along with the guides on how to use them. The solutions were too convenient, and I was ready to use them and have things up and running instead of producing my own solutions – maybe not everyone needs to be able to build a site. This feeling was only enhanced as the evolution of agentic AI and LLMs. I would use them to study on how to work with the site. Trivial questions like "how do I change the background color?" or "how do I add a tab?" were answered instantly, and what was "worse" was that the agent would also make the change in seconds, making the effort of learning it feel futile.

I slightly regret to say that to me this page doesn't fully contain what motivated me to have one, a certain show of skill. Things were learned while making it of course, but it is hard to say that this template based AI assisted site is be "better" for me than the possible Squarespace one. I also cannot claim to fully have made this site myself, which would have been nice from a hiring perspective.

So, maybe the treasure wasn't the skills we learned on the way but the actual treasure at the end of the road. I now have this site, which was part of the goal, and to be honest I think it is better this way (if I wouldn't, this page likely would not be online and readable to you). I could spend time on learning many different things, but sometimes it is OK for me to take a ready solution so that I can focus on the more important things. In this case the more important things are having a platform to write to and to document things.
